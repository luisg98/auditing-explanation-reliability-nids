\begin{abstract}
Post-hoc explanations can support audits of neural intrusion detectors, but
generating an attribution does not establish that it is reliable evidence.
We examine reproducibility, functional response and outcome dependence on
corrected CICIDS2017 and NF-ToN-IoT-V2 binary classification, using MLP and
1D-CNN models, five training seeds and five explainers. We separate stochastic
recomputation, numerical sensitivity and deterministic controls, reporting
identifiable coverage alongside rank and top-10 agreement. Functional response
compares attribution-guided deletion/insertion with displacement-matched random
controls on a common panel under three replacement operators. LIME rank
correlations range from .766 to .879; high conditional agreement for other
methods can conceal non-identifiable top-10 selections. For ToN-IoT, IG insertion
effects change from positive under median replacement to negative under
conditional Gaussian replacement in both architectures. An exploratory
correct/error analysis is limited to three deterministic methods and
explicitly reports scarce false-negative support. The results support
condition-specific auditing of frozen models, not an operator-independent
ranking of explainers, validated alert triage, or a guarantee of IDS safety.
\end{abstract}
